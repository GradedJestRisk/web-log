# Handling contractor relationship

## Why ?

I've been a contractor for 15 years now, on time of this writing. When faced with people landing in this system, trying to do a good job and wondering why so many conflicts arise, I want to share what I learned so they don't waste as much time as me. 

Shortly put, I'm not that greedy about money and power. I nevertheless found myself crying in my manager's office, because I ended trapped in an unhealthy relationship with my client. The client harassed me to cancel my well-ahead planned 2-weeks holiday, so I can work on a task which was impossible to complete. It doesn't matter that I raise alerts to my manager for months, mentioning the client's threat to cancel my holidays. My manager simply told me that was not the way things must work, but he told me to obey the client and cancel my vacation. So, handling contractor relationship implies shielding you from both sides.

## The crux

When working in IT in time when business goes well, in places where there's plenty of opportunity and if your knowledge and skills roughly match existing codebase, the power is balanced between you and the company you're employed, but you may believe the company has more power. Don't forget about this.

This idea is somewhat counter-intuitive, at last to me. As the hierarchy has absolute power over its employees (unless she does some grossly illegal action, or unless you resign), how can an employee have some power ? How can he be free ? I'll talk about it in detail, but let me quote from orthodox (sociology) and unorthodox (lesbian) sources to start with.  

> Nobody can make you feel bad about what you do unless you cooperate. Be proud — if you need help being proud, ask for it. 

Pat Califia, Lesbian S/M Safety Manual

> Le pouvoir d'un individu ou d'un groupe est fonction de l'ampleur de la zone d'incertitude que l'imprévisibilité de son comportement lui permet de contrôler face à ses partenaires.

L'acteur et le système, Michel Crozier
